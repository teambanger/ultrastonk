## System Implementation (12 Points)

### Functionality: Correct EMAs and Buy/Sell Advisories

**Exceptional case:** On the event-time-ordered 8 November reference, the pipeline matched the independent oracle on **377,966 normalized records**, with no missing, extra, numeric, duplicate, or wrong-direction errors, including the final window. The comparison passed across ETR, FR, and NL. A separate reference slice matched **11,608** oracle records. The live browser demonstration shows actual Flink BUY/SELL/BUY advisories with the triggering windows and EMA values; the controlled verifier matched **148** committed records and checked three live subscribers.

**Evidence:** [Ordered-day comparison](../benchmarks/evidence/full-week/reference-day08/comparison.json) · [reference-slice manifest](../benchmarks/evidence/reference-slice/manifest.json) · [hand-worked oracle and comparator](../benchmarks/query-truth.md) · [operator tests](../flink/src/test/java/fi/ultrastonk/PriceIndicatorsTest.java) · [advisory verifier](../benchmarks/evidence/live-advisory/README.md) · [live browser screenshot](../benchmarks/evidence/live-advisory/browser-live.png) and [frames](../benchmarks/evidence/live-advisory/browser-frames.json).

**Qualification:** Exact complete-input correctness was demonstrated for the labeled reference runs, not the whole week in capture order. The [capture-order gate](../benchmarks/evidence/full-week/capture-order/gate.json) failed on late exclusions and oracle agreement under the configured 5 s disorder budget. The browser demonstration uses synthetic input and accelerated 1 s windows; it demonstrates delivery and interpretation, not exact advisories for every real symbol over the week.

### Performance: Whole-Week Processing and Latency Under Load

**Exceptional case:** The bounded capture-order run accounted for all **289,049,752 emitted rows** from seven files. Emitter publication took **530.4 s**; the subsequent bounded Flink job took **428.0 s** (approximately **675,301 input rows/s**, derived), separate from the **616.3 s** output/audit dump. At the recorded five-minute peak, one warm 1× Historical replay offered **1,223,873 rows** (about **4,080 raw rows/s**) and delivered **195,061 expected price records** exactly to committed Kafka and WebSocket. Enqueue-to-WebSocket p50/p95/p99 was **5.06/9.06/9.22 s**; the measured committed-to-WebSocket hop was **15.5/104/134 ms** at the same percentiles.

**Evidence:** [Whole-week source accounting](../benchmarks/evidence/full-week/capture-order/source.json) and [stage timings](../benchmarks/evidence/full-week/capture-order/timings.json) · [recorded-peak fixture](../benchmarks/evidence/live-peak/slices/peak-warm-manifest.json) · [live-run summary](../benchmarks/evidence/live-peak/summary.json) and [latency method](live-peak.md#latency-p50p95p99-with-sample-sizes-two-paths-reported-independently) · [capture-paced pilot](capture-pilot-evidence.md) (supporting method; headline numbers retained in prose only).

**Interpretation:** The historical price p99 was already **9.206 s** at the committed Kafka reader. In a [controlled synthetic comparison](live-peak.md#synthetic-price-latency-comparison), shortening checkpoints from 10 s to 1 s cut WebSocket price p99 from **10.004 s to 1.028 s** with the watermark allowance fixed at 5 s; shortening only that allowance left p99 at **9.991 s**. Prices do not wait for window closure. The gateway figure is an observation gap, not a network-only measurement.

**Qualification:** The 428 s job window is a preloaded processing measurement, not a live end-to-end result. The synthetic comparison does not measure historical peak latency under the shorter cadence or explain advisory timing. One warm peak run and missing Kafka/gateway consumer-group lag do not establish stable live latency across the week.

### Creative Explorations: Measured Design Trade-offs and Visualization

**Exceptional case:** The matched **DROP versus GRACE** experiment changes only the lateness policy across two repetitions per arm. In the scripted fixture, GRACE recovered one advisory and two EMA values relative to complete-input truth, at the cost of a **30 s policy finalization delay** and larger checkpoints. The replay experiments quantify the consequences of captured versus event-time order; the fixed-key long-tail experiment shows how concentration loads a Kafka partition. The browser chart makes the output interpretable by pairing each live advisory with its triggering window and EMA values.

**Strategy matrix and synthetic scenarios:** The [policy design](policies.md) crosses three deduplication strategies (`NONE`, `EXACT`, `TIMESTAMP`) with three late-event strategies (`DROP`, `SIDE_OUTPUT`, `GRACE`). The [October validation notes](validation.md#implementation-validation) record that all nine combinations passed a short price-multiset smoke test (500 originals/s, 3 measured seconds, one worker, one repeat). That checks price delivery under the configured deduplication rules, not which late policy gives the best EMA/advisory accuracy, latency, or resource cost. [Operator tests](../flink/src/test/java/fi/ultrastonk/PoliciesTest.java) separately show that `EXACT` preserves same-timestamp corrections that `TIMESTAMP` suppresses, and that `SIDE_OUTPUT` audits excluded ticks. The matched DROP/GRACE run above is the quantified late-policy comparison.

The [standalone scenario emitter](../benchmarks/synthetic-emitter/README.md#scenarios) simulates bursts and pauses, duplicates, same-time corrections, real publication delays, backdating, and malformed values or tombstones. Its fault trace preserves the injected input labels for comparison with downstream results. The October validation notes report Kafka acknowledgement of 300 clean records and a separate 2,000-record quality run covering all six malformed/tombstone fault modes. That is producer-to-Kafka validation, not downstream correctness. The strategy matrix, capacity and growth runs, and checkpoint sweep use the separate [benchmark driver](benchmarking.md), not those presets. The DROP/GRACE comparison uses its own watermark-synchronized fixture.

**Checkpoint cadence trade-off:** A [four-interval sweep](live-peak.md#checkpoint-interval-sweep) held the 5 s watermark allowance fixed at 1,000 synthetic originals/s. Each of eight trials delivered all **40,000** prices. WebSocket price p99 was **9.989–9.998 s** at 10 s checkpoints, **2.521–2.523 s** at 2.5 s, **1.027–1.030 s** at 1 s, and **0.536–0.556 s** at 500 ms. Shorter checkpoints made committed prices visible sooner but increased Flink-reported checkpoint bytes per second. **Recommendation:** Start at **1 s** for interactive price delivery: it reduced p99 by about 90% versus 10 s, while 500 ms saved another 0.47–0.49 s at roughly **2.3×** the checkpoint bytes/s of 1 s. Consider **2.5 s** if lower checkpoint work matters more than a roughly 2.52 s price p99.

**Evidence:** [Machine-readable checkpoint results](../benchmarks/evidence/live-peak/checkpoint-interval-sweep.json) · [DROP/GRACE comparison and reproduction](../benchmarks/evidence/drop-vs-grace/README.md) · [machine-readable policy comparison](../benchmarks/evidence/drop-vs-grace/comparison.json) · [ordered versus capture-order results](../benchmarks/evidence/full-week/summary.json) · [long-tail experiment](long-tail.md) · [browser evidence](../benchmarks/evidence/live-advisory/browser-live.png) and [browser implementation](../gateway/public/index.html) · [predictive volume/resource model](capacity-model.md).

**Qualification:** These synthetic experiments do not establish week-wide performance or trading profitability. The strategy smoke-test result is documented in prose; its raw matrix artifacts are not in the evidence pack. The checkpoint sweep measured completed checkpoint counts and reported byte rates, not physical I/O, CPU, or recovery time. Validate the recommendation at the recorded peak before changing deployment defaults.

## Final Report (12 Points)

### System Design and Architecture: How the Pipeline Meets the Requirements

**Exceptional case to make in the report:** Diagram the Java CSV emitter → Kafka input → Flink validation, watermarks and keyed state → committed Kafka output → WebSocket gateway; show the rejected/audit topic alongside the main path. Explain why partitioning by ticker preserves per-symbol state, why event-time watermarks handle bounded disorder, and why read-committed Kafka separates checkpointed processing from subscription fan-out. Explain the finite-replay end-offset mechanism, deployment/resource limits, and where additional workers can or cannot help.

**Evidence:** [Integration and topic boundaries](module-integration.md#integration-interfaces) · [Compose topology](../compose.yaml) and [deployment overlays](../deploy/) · [Flink pipeline source](../flink/src/main/java/fi/ultrastonk/PricePipeline.java) · [gateway Kafka consumer](../gateway/src/kafka-source.js) and [gateway protocol](../gateway/README.md) · [recovery and rescaling](../README.md#recovery-and-rescaling) · [monitoring and telemetry](telemetry.md).

**Qualification:** Explain the local deployment and recovery boundaries. Do not claim cross-host or JobManager high availability, or exactly-once delivery to browsers on reconnect, from the transactional Kafka sink alone.

### Implementation Explanation: Event Handling, Metadata, and Query State

**Exceptional case to make in the report:** Include a compact field-to-behavior table. Show how raw instrument ID and security type normalize to ticker and `sec_type`, how trading/system timestamps are selected, and how `Last` drives price processing. Valid quote and housekeeping rows without a positive `Last` can advance watermarks without updating EMAs; malformed input follows the rejection path. Explain exchange suffix and security-type usage as implemented, not as an assumed external metadata join. For both queries, walk one example from a five-minute tumbling-window close through zero-initialized EMA38/EMA100 (`alpha = 2/(N+1)`) and the sign change that emits BUY or SELL. Explain what is stored per ticker, what closes the final window, and what the gateway sends. Name the languages, runtime versions, container tooling, and benchmark host/resource configuration actually used.

**Evidence:** [CSV fields and timestamp fallback](../event-emitter/src/main/java/org/ultrastonk/eventemitter/DataLoader.java) · [normalization and validation](../flink/src/main/java/fi/ultrastonk/EventJson.java) and [parser tests](../flink/src/test/java/fi/ultrastonk/EventJsonTest.java) · [window/EMA/crossover implementation](../flink/src/main/java/fi/ultrastonk/PriceIndicators.java) · [worked crossover](../benchmarks/query-truth.md) · [Kafka wire formats](../README.md#kafka-contracts) · [policy and state handling](policies.md) · [benchmarks and resource configuration](benchmarking.md).

### Evaluation and Performance: Correctness, Throughput, Latency, and Resources

**Exceptional case to make in the report:** Combine complementary experiments rather than treating one run as proof of everything. Show the **ordered-day exact match next to the whole-week capture-order gate failure**, then the measured peak with stage-separated latency, then the synthetic load/worker and growth results. Report per-exchange coverage and comparisons, measured CPU/throttling for the resource sweep, and the fixed-key long-tail results. For each table, give the input unit, run mode, sample size, resources, and repetition count.

**Evidence:** [Dataset mix and burst profile](dataset-profile.md#full-week) · [whole-week correctness and timed stages](../benchmarks/evidence/full-week/README.md) · [peak delivery comparison](../benchmarks/evidence/live-peak/comparison-warm-1x-r1.json) and [oracle discrepancy classification](../benchmarks/evidence/live-peak/comparison-warm-1x-r1-classification.json) · [capacity rules and preserved attempts](../benchmarks/evidence/sustainable-limit/README.md) · [long-tail results](../benchmarks/evidence/long-tail/summary.json) · [subscriber resource series summary](../benchmarks/evidence/subscribers/summary.json) · [audit and remaining evidence gaps](evidence-audit.md).

**Qualification:** CPU and memory were measured for selected runs; this is a CPU-based deployment, not a GPU experiment. The exchange result is a mixed-workload measurement plus per-exchange correctness, not an exchange-by-exchange throughput sweep. Report the single repetitions, missing peak lag telemetry, and unexplained checkpoint failures. The 1,000/s capacity verdicts are backlog-rule failures despite exact delivery; do not silently discard them or claim a measured Sustainable limit.

### Academic Writing: Dataset-Led, Clear, and ACM-Compliant

**Exceptional case to make in the report:** Start with why the workload is difficult: **289,116,808** rows across seven files and three exchanges, with the top 100 symbols accounting for **58.53%** of rows, a bursty recorded peak, and only about **20.55%** provisionally trade-like rows. Connect these properties to the design and experiment choices. Use consistent citations, units and captions; label results as measured, derived, or predicted and distinguish recorded, ordered, and synthetic inputs.

**Evidence and submission checks:** [Dataset analysis and definitions](dataset-profile.md) · [official dataset DOI](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.6382482) and [challenge paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13237) · [figure/table inventory](report-evidence.md#9-figures-and-tables-for-the-45-page-acm-report) · [reproducibility index](../benchmarks/evidence/README.md#how-to-reproduce-the-whole-pack). Use the **ACM proceedings template**, verify the rendered report is **4–5 pages**, and include the [GitHub repository link](https://github.com/teambanger/ultrastonk) with access to the cited revision. This sheet is not the final report; writing quality and compliance can only be checked in the submitted document.
